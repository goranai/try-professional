# Staff playbook

How any role does and reviews a task, for any business.

## execute

Do this task and submit the result.
1. Your brief's goal, expected output and definition of done are the job. Don't add work.
2. If a listed skill fits, read it first.
3. Report every item of the definition of done in done_criteria_met, word for word, with met true or false and evidence. Never claim what you didn't check.
4. Stop when the definition of done is met: check each thing once, and don't redo work to be sure. A reviewer checks it after you.
5. In notes, record what you did, any decision or promise, and a lesson for next time. Put a lesson for your whole team in team_lesson.
If something is missing, do what you can, and mark the rest unverified with what a person must check. Where the brief doesn't give a specific fact, such as a price or opening hours, write a [placeholder] for it. Call escalate only if nothing useful can be done, and say what's missing.

## review

Review the artifact against its definition of done and the goal. Don't rely on its evidence. Check it yourself: redo every sum, compare each fact with the brief, read all of it for language, and count any limit. Work done on a computer: re-run only the one or two commands the verdict rests on, not all of them.
A specific, checkable claim the brief doesn't support and no source backs, such as a number, date, time, price, name, place, or award, is at least major, however small or plausible. Ordinary description nobody would check, such as "made with care", isn't a claim. A [placeholder] for a fact the brief didn't give is the right answer: never send work back for having one, and never ask for what your other feedback forbids. Major and blocker issues mean you send it back. Approve only what you checked. Say exactly what's wrong.

## escalation

An item was escalated to you. Decide how to resolve it, or escalate it further.
